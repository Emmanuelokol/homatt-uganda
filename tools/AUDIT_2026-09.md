# Clinical safety audit — New Treatment, one-tap packages, billing

What was checked, what was found, what was fixed, and what is still wrong.
Every figure here comes from a check that runs in `offline-test/`, against the
real databases in a real browser — not from reading the code.

---

## 1. The one that could have hurt someone

**Typing "Uncomplicated Malaria" produced the severe, inpatient protocol —
pre-ticked and pre-billed.**

All thirteen medicines arrived ticked: artemether/lumefantrine (right), and
with it **artesunate, quinine, phenobarbital IM, furosemide, IV diazepam,
Dextrose 5%, sodium bicarbonate 8.4% and sodium chloride 0.9%**. A nurse who
tapped through would have set up an outpatient with three days of tablets for
an IV severe-malaria regimen, and charged them for it.

Two causes, both fixed:

1. **Everything was ticked by default.** `d.selected = group !== 'other'` — the
   intent was "arrives ready, remove what you are not giving", which is right
   for a two-drug package and dangerous for a thirteen-drug one.
2. **The section borrows its neighbour's page.** "Uncomplicated Malaria" (p.165)
   has *no medicines of its own* in the parsed database; the app borrows
   "Complicated/Severe Malaria" (p.166), which has all thirteen.

**Now:** the default follows the guideline's own ranking and the severity
chosen — **one** treatment drug, the book's first choice; alternatives and
second line shown but not ticked; anything into a vein, and anything
supportive, only for severe or critical. Uncomplicated malaria ticks
artemether/lumefantrine and nothing else. Typhoid ticks ciprofloxacin, not
chloramphenicol. Severe malaria still brings the whole protocol back.

The trade-off is deliberate: a genuine combination (TB, H. pylori) now needs a
tap per drug. Under-ticking costs a tap; over-ticking gives a patient medicine
they do not need.

## 2. Dosing arithmetic — three shapes, not one

`audit-doses.js` walks **all 535 conditions** and checks every drug line. The
app dispenses in three shapes and each has its own sum:

| shape | example | rule |
|---|---|---|
| unit | tablets, capsules | qty = times/day × days |
| container | syrup, cream, drops | qty = bottles, **not** doses |
| given here | drip, ampoule, rectal tube | a small number, given at the clinic |

Two real faults found and fixed:

- **`diazepam 2.5 mg RECTAL` was dispensed as tablets** — 2/day for 5 days,
  qty 10. Ten rectal tubes for one dose given at the clinic. `isGivenHere()`
  did not look at the guideline's parsed *route*, only at the dosage text.
- **A drug recovered from the guideline's prose always got 2 × 5 = 10**, even
  when it was a 500 ml bag. Saline and hydrocortisone were arriving as qty 10
  in the anaphylaxis and emergency packages.

346 conditions produce a package; the arithmetic now holds for every line in
all of them.

## 3. The bill did not follow the list

**"Total Charged" was UGX 0 and never moved.** Removing a lab test or crossing
out a medicine changed nothing, because the total was never derived from them —
it was two boxes someone typed into. A clinician could take four drugs off a
package and still charge for them.

**Now** every re-render reprices from what is actually ticked: lab from the
clinic's own price list (shared with the wizard so a test costs the same figure
wherever it is added), medicines from the clinic's own shelf price × quantity.
Ticking, unticking, changing a quantity and removing a test all move the total,
both ways. A medicine the clinic has never priced counts as zero and **says
so** rather than quietly making itself free.

## 4. Who the patient is

- **Sex.** 83 conditions can only happen to one sex (76 female-only, 7
  male-only), derived from the UCG's own chapters (gynaecology, family
  planning, obstetrics) plus title keywords for the ones filed elsewhere —
  ectopic pregnancy under emergencies, mastitis under skin. Until the sex is
  recorded **all 83 are held back**, and the screen says how many and why. A
  blank field must not quietly widen the differential.
- **Age.** Banded paediatric (<5), child (5–12), adult (>12); months accepted,
  because a Ugandan mother gives a baby's age in months.
- **A child never gets an adult dose.** Every figure in the UCG medicines table
  is an adult dose. For a patient under 13 the package arrives with **nothing
  ticked** and points at the children's weight-band table
  (Guidelines → Children → Child doses).

## 5. ICD-10 codes — removed, not fixed

The `icd10` column in `uganda_clinical_guidelines_2023.db` is unreliable. The
parser took the first ICD-like token on the page, which usually belongs to a
neighbouring condition. Of twelve well-known conditions checked, **nine were
wrong**:

| condition | database says | which is actually |
|---|---|---|
| Peptic ulcer disease | K86.0 | chronic pancreatitis |
| Typhoid fever | A75.9 | typhus |
| Appendicitis | K85 | acute pancreatitis |
| Acute pancreatitis | K92.2 | GI haemorrhage |
| Meningitis | B45.1 | cryptococcosis |
| Measles | A80.3 | polio |

351 of 535 conditions carry one. The chip is now **hidden** on the Guidelines
screen: a wrong ICD-10 goes onto forms, claims and returns, and is worse than
none. It stays hidden until the codes are rebuilt and verified against the real
classification — which is a data job, not a display job, and is **not** done.

## Still wrong — the next piece of work

**About one UCG condition in six carries another condition's clinical
features.** This is the same page-range slicing that gave uncomplicated malaria
the severe protocol. Judged by hand on a random sample of 20 conditions, 3–4
were clearly wrong:

- **Hairy Leukoplakia** — features describe Kaposi's sarcoma
  ("painless purplish swelling on the skin")
- **Nodding Disease** — features describe migraine ("aura: visual or sensory
  symptoms preceding the headache")
- **Painful Scrotal Swelling** — features describe **conjunctivitis**
  ("purulent discharge from one or both eyes within 30 days from birth")
- **Prostatitis** — features describe renal colic

This degrades the half of the suggestion engine that reads the UCG. The other
half — the WHO differential tables — is verified at 98% and is unaffected.

The fix is to rebuild `uganda_clinical_guidelines_2023.db` with section
boundaries that follow the book's headings rather than its page numbers. That
touches all 535 conditions, every one-tap package and the whole test suite, so
it is its own piece of work rather than a patch. **It has not been done.**

A smaller known gap: malaria and urinary tract infection have no parsed
"Investigations" section, so the suggestion screen offers no confirming test
for either — the two commonest tests a Ugandan clinic runs.
